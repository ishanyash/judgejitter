How to read this

Flip rate. For one item, the share of its calls that disagree with its most common verdict. With 5 calls the possible values are 0%, 20% and 40%.

Self-agreement. Fleiss' kappa treats each call as a rater and corrects for agreement by chance. For 1 to 5 scores the report uses the one-way intraclass correlation, ICC(1,1), because repeated calls are interchangeable rather than distinct raters. Kappa drops when nearly every verdict is the same; read it next to the flip rate.

Rerun intervals. Each item's chance of a pass on one call is taken as its observed share of passing calls. The report then simulates 4,000 reruns: it draws k calls per item, takes the majority, and averages over items. The interval holds the middle 95% of those pass rates. Items with no flips in 5 calls are treated as never flipping, so the intervals are a lower bound on the noise.

Recommended calls per item. The smallest odd k whose rerun interval is at most 2.0 points wide. Odd numbers avoid ties in the vote.
